Porcelain Paver Patios

Porcelain Paver Patios and Pool Decks in Minnesota

Porcelain pavers give a patio or pool deck the cleanest surface we install: large formats, tight joints, wood and stone textures that do not fade, and a body so dense that wine, grill grease and sunscreen wipe off instead of soaking in. We design and install porcelain across the Twin Cities on the same engineered base we build under every patio.

Large-Format Porcelain · Stain Resistant · Built for Freeze-Thaw

Outdoor porcelain is a kiln-fired paver, typically 20 mm (about 3/4 inch) thick, made for ground contact and foot traffic. It is harder and denser than a concrete paver, so it resists scratching, fading and staining, and it absorbs almost no water, which is what matters most in a freeze-thaw climate. The trade-off is cost and the skill it takes to cut and set it cleanly. Porcelain shows every flaw in the base and every lazy cut, so it belongs with a crew that lays it regularly.

We build porcelain two ways. On-grade, the paver sits on a bedding layer over a deep, compacted aggregate base with a solid edge restraint, the same foundation under every patio we build. On pedestals, the pavers float on adjustable supports for elevated or precisely leveled decks. Either way the finished surface is flat, the joints are tight and consistent, and the pool deck or patio looks like it belongs to the house. Why It Matters

Why Porcelain Earns Its Premium in Minnesota

Freeze-thaw damage starts with water getting into a material and expanding. Porcelain absorbs almost none, so built on a proper base it comes through our winters without the surface spalling or the color washing out. It also does not stain, which around a pool or a grill is the difference between a deck that looks new in year five and one that needs sealing and scrubbing. The honest downside is cost: porcelain runs about $10 to $15 per square foot more than concrete pavers installed. For a tighter budget, concrete pavers are still an excellent patio, and we build plenty of them.

How We Work

How We Build a Porcelain Patio or Pool Deck

The base is the same engineered foundation we build under every patio. The difference is in the layout, the cuts and the joints.

See It in the Showroom

Porcelain samples from Belgard, Tile Tech, RCP, Geoceramica and MSI are on the wall at our Lake Elmo showroom. Photos flatten texture; in person you can compare wood, stone and concrete looks side by side.

3D Design and Layout

Format and pattern are decided in the design, not on site. Large formats need a layout that minimizes cuts at the house, the pool coping and the steps.

Excavate and Build the Base

Excavation to depth, a compacted aggregate base graded to drain, and a solid edge restraint. Porcelain is unforgiving of a soft base, so this step is never rushed.

Set and Cut

Pavers set to a flat, consistent plane with tight joints. Every cut is made with a wet saw so edges are clean, including the radius cuts around a pool.

Joint, Clean and Walk Through

Joint sand rated for porcelain, a final clean, and a walkthrough on how to care for it, which is mostly a hose.

What Affects the Cost

What a Porcelain Patio Costs in the Twin Cities

Installed, porcelain pavers run $55 to $77 per square foot in the Twin Cities, about $10 to $15 per square foot more than concrete pavers. A 350 square foot patio lands roughly $19,000 to $27,000; an 800 square foot pool deck roughly $44,000 to $62,000, before fence, steps, lighting and plantings.

As a complete project, Poolside on the Hill in Woodbury was about $120,000 for the porcelain deck, boulder retaining and steps, aluminum fence, plantings, lighting and lawn around a pool installed by our sister company Blaiser Pools (the pool itself is separate).

  • Format and finishLarge slabs and plank formats cost more than standard sizes; premium textures cost more than basic ones.
  • Cuts and patternCurves, pool radii and diagonal layouts mean more wet-saw time and more waste.
  • On-grade vs pedestalPedestal systems add hardware but can skip some excavation on elevated decks.
  • Base and accessPoor soil, steep grades and tight access add excavation and labor, as with any patio.

FAQ

Common Porcelain Paver Patios Questions

How much does a porcelain paver patio cost in Minnesota? +
Installed, porcelain runs $55 to $77 per square foot in the Twin Cities, roughly $10 to $15 more per square foot than concrete pavers. A typical 350 square foot patio lands around $19,000 to $27,000 before features like steps, seat walls or lighting. See our porcelain cost guide for the full breakdown.
Do porcelain pavers hold up to Minnesota winters? +
Yes. Porcelain is so dense it absorbs almost no water, and water absorption is what drives freeze-thaw damage. Built on a properly compacted, well-drained base, a porcelain patio resists the spalling and surface damage that can affect more porous materials over many winters.
Are porcelain pavers slippery around a pool? +
Not when the right finish is chosen. Outdoor porcelain comes in textured, slip-resistant finishes made for wet areas, and that is what we specify for pool decks. Light colors also stay more comfortable underfoot in July than dark surfaces.
Porcelain vs concrete pavers, which should I choose? +
Concrete pavers cost less and come in a huge range of styles, and we install a lot of them. Porcelain costs more but gives you a denser, more stain-resistant, more fade-resistant surface with very low upkeep and the cleanest modern look. If the patio hosts dinners and pool days and you want it to look new for years, porcelain is the upgrade. See our paver patio page for the concrete and natural stone options.
How do you install porcelain pavers? +
Either on-grade, set on a bedding layer over a deep compacted aggregate base with an edge restraint, or on a pedestal system where the pavers float on adjustable supports for elevated or precisely leveled decks. On-grade is the most common for backyard patios and pool decks.
What porcelain brands do you install? +
Belgard, Tile Tech, RCP, Geoceramica and MSI. Samples are on the wall at our Lake Elmo showroom, so you can compare textures and colors in person before you decide.
Do porcelain patios need sealing? +
No. Porcelain does not need sealing. Maintenance is a rinse with a hose and the occasional sweep. Spills sit on the surface and wipe off, which is one of the main reasons homeowners choose it.
