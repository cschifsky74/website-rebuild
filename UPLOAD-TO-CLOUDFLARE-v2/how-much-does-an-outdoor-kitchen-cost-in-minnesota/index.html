How Much Does an Outdoor Kitchen Cost in Minnesota? [2026 Data]

By Carson Schifsky, Owner, Schifsky Companies

A professionally built outdoor kitchen in Minnesota costs $20,000 to $35,000 for a grill island, $40,000 to $80,000 for a full kitchen with counters, storage, a sink and a bar, and $80,000 to $150,000 or more for a premium kitchen with a pizza oven, refrigeration, a roof structure and lighting. The biggest levers are how many utilities you run, the appliances you choose, and whether the kitchen is part of a larger outdoor living project.

Below is the full 2026 breakdown from a licensed Twin Cities design-build contractor, with real project numbers and the Minnesota-specific details that separate a kitchen that lasts from one that cracks in three winters.

Outdoor kitchen cost at a glance

  • Grill island with counter: $20,000 to $35,000 installed.
  • Full outdoor kitchen with counters, storage, sink and bar: $40,000 to $80,000.
  • Premium kitchen with pizza oven, fridge, roof and lighting: $80,000 to $150,000+.
  • Appliances alone typically run $5,000 to $30,000+ depending on brand and how many.
  • Running gas, water and electrical adds $3,000 to $15,000 depending on distance from the house.
  • A kitchen is almost always built as part of a patio or full backyard project, not on its own.
  • Best value window: pick appliances over winter and book by February for a summer build.

What actually drives the price

Utilities are the first big variable. A grill on a propane tank needs nothing. A kitchen with a natural gas grill, a sink, a fridge and lighting needs a licensed plumber, a gas fitter and an electrician, and the farther the kitchen sits from the house, the more trenching and pipe that takes.

Appliances are the second. A quality built-in grill starts around $2,500 and premium brands run $8,000 to $15,000 on their own. Add a side burner, a kamado, an outdoor-rated fridge, an ice maker and a pizza oven and appliances alone can pass $30,000.

Structure and finish are the third. Masonry and natural stone veneer cost more than stucco or manufactured stone. Granite, quartzite and porcelain counters cost more than concrete. A roof, a pergola or an aluminum louvered pergola over the kitchen turns it into a room and adds meaningfully to the budget.

Why Minnesota changes how a kitchen has to be built

An outdoor kitchen is a heavy masonry object sitting on ground that freezes several feet deep every winter. If it sits on a thin slab or a shallow base it will heave, and the counters, veneer and grout lines will crack. We build kitchens on the same deep, compacted, well-drained base system we use under our paver patios, or on frost footings when the design calls for it, so the kitchen and the patio move together and stay flat.

Materials matter too. Cabinet boxes need to be masonry, stainless or marine-grade polymer, not wood. Appliances need to be rated for outdoor use. Water lines need shutoffs and drains so the kitchen can be winterized in October and brought back in April. None of that is expensive relative to the kitchen, but all of it is the difference between a kitchen that lasts and one you replace.

Real Twin Cities outdoor kitchens

Our Gather Round the Fire project in Hugo, a $295,000 full backyard, includes a stone outdoor kitchen with a built-in grill and kamado alongside an outdoor fireplace, plunge spa and pergola-covered dining. The Modern Revival in Woodbury, at $235,000, pairs an outdoor kitchen and bar with a linear fire table and built-in seating. In both, the kitchen is one piece of a complete outdoor living design, which is how most kitchens we build come together.

Do you need a permit for an outdoor kitchen?

In most Twin Cities cities the masonry island itself does not require a permit, but the utilities do. Natural gas, electrical and plumbing each need a permit pulled by a licensed trade, and a roof structure over the kitchen almost always requires a building permit. We handle the permits and coordinate the trades as part of the project so you are not managing four contractors.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an outdoor kitchen cost in Minnesota? +
A professionally built outdoor kitchen in Minnesota runs $20,000 to $35,000 for a grill island with counter space, $40,000 to $80,000 for a full kitchen with counters, storage, a sink and a bar, and $80,000 to $150,000 or more for a premium kitchen with a pizza oven, refrigeration, a roof structure and lighting.
What is the cheapest way to build an outdoor kitchen? +
A single grill island with a built-in grill, stone or masonry veneer, and a stretch of counter is the most affordable permanent option, usually $20,000 to $35,000 installed. It gives you the built-in look and a real work surface without running water or gas lines across the yard.
Do outdoor kitchens hold up in Minnesota winters? +
Yes, when they are built on a proper base with the right materials. We build kitchens on a compacted, well-drained base or frost footings so they do not heave, use masonry, stone, or marine-grade cabinetry, and specify appliances rated for outdoor use. Water lines are installed with shutoffs and drains so they can be winterized every fall.
Do I need a permit for an outdoor kitchen in Minnesota? +
The masonry itself usually does not require a permit, but running natural gas, electrical, or plumbing to the kitchen does, and a roof structure over it almost always does. We pull the permits and coordinate the licensed trades as part of the project.
Is an outdoor kitchen worth it in Minnesota? +
For homeowners who cook and entertain outside, yes. A built-in kitchen turns a patio into the place people gather from May through October and, with a fire feature, well into the shoulder seasons. It is also one of the outdoor features buyers notice most in a premium Twin Cities home.
How long does it take to build an outdoor kitchen? +
The build itself usually takes two to four weeks once the patio base is in, depending on utilities and the size of the kitchen. Design and appliance lead times matter more than build time, so we finalize selections over the winter for a spring or summer install.
