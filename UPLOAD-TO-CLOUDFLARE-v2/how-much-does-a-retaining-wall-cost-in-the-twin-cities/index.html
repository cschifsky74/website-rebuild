How Much Does a Retaining Wall Cost in the Twin Cities? [2026 Data]

By Carson Schifsky, Owner, Schifsky Companies

A professionally built retaining wall in the Twin Cities costs $65 to $85 per square foot of wall face for boulder walls, $85 to $120 per square foot for segmental block, and $120 and up for stacked natural stone. Most residential wall projects land between $15,000 and $60,000, and large tiered lakefront walls can run into the hundreds of thousands. The two things that move the number most are the wall system you choose and what has to happen underground to keep it standing through Minnesota winters.

Below is the full 2026 breakdown from a licensed Minnesota contractor that has been building walls since 1989, when retaining walls were the first thing this company did.

Retaining wall cost at a glance

  • Boulder walls run $65 to $85 per square foot of wall face, installed.
  • Segmental block walls run $85 to $120 per square foot, installed.
  • Stacked natural stone runs $120+ per square foot, installed.
  • Most residential wall projects land $15,000 to $60,000.
  • Walls over 4 feet need engineering and a permit in most Twin Cities cities, typically adding $2,500 to $5,000.
  • Drainage and reinforcement you never see are the biggest driver of whether the wall lasts.
  • Best value window: design over winter and book by February for a spring build.

“Square foot of wall face” means the height times the length of the visible wall. A wall 4 feet tall and 50 feet long is 200 square feet of face, so a block version lands roughly $17,000 to $24,000 before steps, lighting, or engineering.

What actually drives the price

Height is the first lever. A wall’s cost does not rise in a straight line with height, because once you pass about 4 feet the wall needs geogrid reinforcement layered back into the soil, a deeper base, and an engineer’s stamp. A 5-foot wall can easily cost twice what a 3-foot wall of the same length does.

Material is the second. Boulders are the most economical because the material is local and set with an excavator. Block costs more per square foot but gives you tight geometry, built-in steps, matching caps, and pillars. Natural stone is the premium finish and takes the most skilled labor to fit.

Drainage and base are where cheap walls cut corners. Every wall we build sits on a compacted aggregate base with clean drainage stone behind it and a perforated drain pipe at the footing. That system is part of our price, and it is the reason our walls stay straight.

Site access and what you are holding back round it out. A wall holding a driveway, a pool deck, or a slope with a house above it carries more load and more engineering than a wall edging a garden bed. Tight backyards where material has to be moved by hand add labor.

Why walls fail in Minnesota

Retaining walls in the Twin Cities rarely fail because of the block. They fail because of water and frost. Clay soil, which is common across the east metro, holds water. When that water freezes it expands, and a wall without drainage stone and a drain pipe behind it gets pushed outward a little more every winter until it bulges or leans. Skipping geogrid on a taller wall does the same thing from the weight of the soil alone.

That is why the most important parts of a wall are the parts you never see. Building them right costs more up front and is the difference between a wall you rebuild in five years and one your kids inherit.

Do you need a permit for a retaining wall?

In most Twin Cities cities, a retaining wall over 4 feet tall, measured from the bottom of the footing to the top of the wall, requires a building permit and a stamped engineering design. Walls of any height near property lines, easements, shorelines, or steep slopes may also need review, and lakefront properties often involve watershed district approval. We confirm the rules with your city and handle the permitting as part of the project.

Retaining walls and the rest of the backyard

Most of the walls we build are not standalone. They hold a grade so a paver patio can be level, they wrap a pool deck, or they become seat walls around a fire feature. Designing the wall together with the patio and landscape, rather than adding it later, almost always saves money, because the excavation, base, and drainage happen once. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a retaining wall cost in the Twin Cities? +
A professionally built retaining wall in the Twin Cities runs $65 to $85 per square foot of wall face for boulder walls, $85 to $120 per square foot for segmental block, and $120 and up for stacked natural stone. Most residential wall projects land between $15,000 and $60,000, with large tiered lakefront walls running well beyond that.
What is the cheapest type of retaining wall in Minnesota? +
Boulder walls are usually the most affordable option at $65 to $85 per square foot, because the material is local and there is no cutting or precise coursing. Block walls cost more per square foot but deliver clean lines, tighter geometry, and integrated steps, caps, and lighting.
Do I need a permit or an engineer for a retaining wall in Minnesota? +
In most Twin Cities cities, a retaining wall over 4 feet tall measured from the bottom of the footing needs a building permit and a stamped engineering design. Walls near property lines, shorelines, or easements can require review at any height. We confirm the rules with your city and handle the permit as part of the project.
Why do retaining walls fail in Minnesota? +
Almost always because of water and frost, not the block. A wall built without a compacted base, clean drainage stone, a perforated drain pipe, and proper geogrid reinforcement will heave and bulge after a few freeze-thaw winters. The parts you never see are what keep the wall straight.
How long does a retaining wall last? +
A properly engineered and drained segmental block or natural stone wall should last 50 years or more in Minnesota. Boulder walls are essentially permanent when set correctly. A wall built without drainage or reinforcement can start to lean in as little as three to five winters.
When is the best time to build a retaining wall in Minnesota? +
Late April through October, once the ground is thawed and dry enough for excavation. Spring books up fastest, so homeowners who finalize design over the winter and lock in a contractor by February get their preferred schedule.
