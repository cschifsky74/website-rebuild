Timber Wall Replacement

Timber and Railroad-Tie Retaining Wall Replacement in Minnesota

Treated-timber and railroad-tie walls were the standard way to hold a Minnesota slope for decades, and most of them are now past their life: rotting at the base, bowing out at the middle, with stairs pulling away and soil washing through. We demolish and haul the old wall, fix the drainage that killed it, and rebuild in block, natural stone or boulders that will hold the grade for the next owner too.

Wood walls fail from the inside. Water sits behind the timbers, the ties rot where they touch the soil, the deadmen that tie the wall back give up, and the wall starts to lean. Once that starts there is no repair that lasts; the fix is a new wall built the way the old one should have been, with drainage rock and drain tile behind it, geogrid or a proper batter for the height, and a material that does not rot. Old railroad ties are creosote-treated and have to be hauled and disposed of properly, which is in our price.

Timber Wall Replacement

A Wall Replacement Includes

  • Demolition and haul-off of the timber or tie wall, including creosote ties
  • Excavation and a compacted base below frost concerns
  • Drainage rock and drain tile behind the new wall
  • Geogrid reinforcement or engineered design where height requires it
  • New stairs, landings and railings where the old ones were
  • Backfill, plantings and lawn restored above and below
Why It Matters

Why Timber Walls Fail and Why the New One Will Not

A timber wall usually has no drainage behind it, so every spring the soil behind it is saturated and heavy, and every winter that water freezes and pushes. The wood rots at the soil line, the deadmen rot with it, and the wall bows. A block, stone or boulder wall built with drainage rock, drain tile and geogrid gets rid of the water instead of holding it, and the material itself does not rot or lose strength. Build it that way and the wall outlasts the house.

How We Work

How We Replace a Failing Wall

Assess the Wall

How tall, how long, what it is holding, where the water comes from, and whether the stairs, fence or patio above it have to come out too.

Design and Engineering

Material, height and tiering decided in the design. Walls over about 4 feet get an engineered design and a permit; we handle both.

Demolish and Haul

The old timbers or ties come out and go to proper disposal. We protect what stays, the lawn, trees and the house, during demolition.

Base, Drainage and Build

Compacted base, the first course set level, drainage rock and drain tile behind, geogrid where specified, then the wall, with stairs built in as we go.

Backfill and Restore

Backfill, grading so water runs where it should, railings, plantings and lawn, and a walkthrough.

Materials

What We Rebuild In

The right material depends on the height, the look of the house and the budget. All three are built with the same drainage.

Segmental Block

Engineered concrete block in a wide range of colors and textures, the most common replacement and the one that handles height best. Runs $85 to $120 per square foot of face.

Natural Stone

Stacked limestone or cut stone for a timeless look, like the White Bear Lake terraces above. $120 and up per square foot of face.

Boulders

Natural fieldstone boulders for a softer, more organic wall on wooded and lake lots, like Dellwood. $65 to $85 per square foot of face.

Tiered Instead of Tall

Two or three shorter terraces with planting beds between them often cost less than one engineered tall wall and look better.

Stairs and Railings

Stone or block steps with landings, and cable or aluminum railings where the drop requires them.

Planted Walls

Block walls that double as planters, the way the Shakopee pool walls were done with hydrangeas and grasses.

What Affects the Cost

What a Timber Wall Replacement Costs

Measured per square foot of visible wall face (height times length), boulder walls run $65 to $85, segmental block $85 to $120, and stacked natural stone $120 and up, installed. Most residential wall projects land between $15,000 and $60,000. Demolition and haul-off of the old wall is priced on top, and so are new stairs and railings.

Walls over about 4 feet typically need an engineered design and a permit, which adds roughly $2,500 to $5,000. Large tiered lake walls like Terraced on White Bear Lake run well beyond the typical range as part of a full property transformation.

  • Height and lengthFace area drives the price; height drives engineering and geogrid.
  • DemolitionHow much wood or tie has to come out and be disposed of, and what is built on top of it.
  • AccessWalls at the back of a sloped lot or at the water mean more hand work and machine time.
  • Stairs and railingsRebuilt stairs, landings and railings are priced separately from the wall.

FAQ

Common Timber Wall Replacement Questions

How do I know my timber retaining wall needs to be replaced? +
Bowing or leaning anywhere along the wall, rot or soft wood at the base, soil or water washing through the face, stairs or a fence above it pulling away, and ties you can push a screwdriver into. Any of those means the wall is failing, and a leaning wood wall cannot be straightened; it has to be rebuilt.
Can a wood retaining wall be repaired instead of replaced? +
Rarely in a way that lasts. Once the timbers and the deadmen behind them rot, replacing a few boards does not restore the strength, and the drainage problem that caused the rot is still there. Replacing the wall with block, stone or boulders built over proper drainage is the repair.
How much does it cost to replace a timber retaining wall? +
Boulder walls run $65 to $85 per square foot of wall face, block $85 to $120, and natural stone $120 and up, installed, with most residential walls landing between $15,000 and $60,000. Demolition, haul-off, new stairs and engineering for walls over about 4 feet are priced on top. See our retaining wall cost guide.
What do you do with the old railroad ties? +
Railroad ties are creosote-treated, so they cannot be burned or left in a brush pile. We haul them to proper disposal as part of the demolition, and that is included in our price.
Does a replacement wall need a permit? +
Walls over about 4 feet typically need an engineered design and a building permit, and some cities set the threshold lower or require one if the wall supports a structure or a slope above it. Requirements vary by city, so we confirm them for your wall and handle the permit.
Can you rebuild the stairs at the same time? +
Yes, and we usually should, because the stairs are tied into the wall. We rebuild them in block or stone with landings and railings where the drop requires them, as we did at the western Wisconsin lake home in the review above.
