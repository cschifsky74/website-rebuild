Fire Pit vs. Outdoor Fireplace Cost in Minnesota [2026 Data]

By Carson Schifsky, Owner, Schifsky Companies

A built-in wood-burning fire pit in Minnesota costs $3,000 to $12,000, a built-in gas fire pit or linear fire table runs $8,000 to $15,000, and a masonry outdoor fireplace runs $25,000 to $60,000 or more. Which one is right depends on how you will use the space, how close it sits to the house, and whether you want a focal point or a gathering spot.

Below is the 2026 breakdown from a licensed Twin Cities design-build contractor, with real project numbers and the reasons a fire feature is the highest-impact addition to a Minnesota backyard.

Fire feature cost at a glance

  • Built-in wood-burning fire pit: $3,000 to $12,000 installed.
  • Built-in gas fire pit or fire table: $8,000 to $15,000 installed, including the gas line.
  • Masonry outdoor fireplace: $25,000 to $60,000+ installed.
  • Seat walls around a fire pit add roughly $4,000 to $12,000 depending on length and material.
  • A fire feature adds two to three months of usable patio season in Minnesota.
  • Fire features are almost always built with a patio, so budget them together.
  • Best value window: design over winter and book by February for a spring build.

What actually drives the price

Fuel is the first decision. Wood is cheaper to build because there is no gas line, burner or permit for the line. Gas costs more up front but is instant, clean and can sit close to the house and under a roof.

Size and material are the second. A 4-foot round pit in block with a cap is the low end. A larger pit in natural stone, a long linear fire table, or a fireplace that stands 7 or 8 feet tall with a chimney is the high end. Natural stone costs more than block with veneer.

What surrounds it is the third. Most fire pits we build come with seat walls, a paver or natural stone landing, and lighting, which is why they are best budgeted as part of a paver patio project instead of on their own.

Which one should you choose?

Choose a wood-burning fire pit if you want the real thing, plan to sit out in the yard rather than on the main patio, and want the lowest cost. Choose a gas fire pit or fire table if it sits close to the house, you want it lit in ten seconds on a Tuesday night, or it will be under a pergola or near a pool. Choose an outdoor fireplace if you want a true focal point, a wind block on an exposed lot, or an outdoor room with a TV, kitchen and seating around it.

A lot of our best backyards have both: a gas feature at the patio and a wood pit out in the landscape.

Why fire features matter more in Minnesota

Our outdoor season is short. A fire feature is the one thing that reliably stretches it: a patio with a fire pit gets used in April and well into November, and a fireplace under a pergola or roof gets used in light snow. For the money, nothing else on a Minnesota patio delivers as many extra evenings outside.

Permits and safety

A wood-burning fire pit usually does not need a building permit, but most Twin Cities cities regulate size, setbacks from structures and property lines, and what you can burn. A gas line requires a permit pulled by a licensed gas fitter. A tall masonry fireplace often needs a building permit and a frost footing. We confirm the rules with your city and handle the permits as part of the project.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a built-in fire pit cost in Minnesota? +
A built-in wood-burning fire pit in the Twin Cities runs $3,000 to $12,000 installed, depending on size, stone and seating. A built-in gas fire pit or linear fire table runs $8,000 to $15,000, which includes the burner, the gas line and the masonry.
How much does an outdoor fireplace cost in Minnesota? +
A masonry outdoor fireplace in the Twin Cities runs $25,000 to $60,000 or more. The range depends on height, natural stone versus block with veneer, wood versus gas, and whether it includes wood boxes, a hearth, seat walls or a built-in TV wall.
Gas or wood: which is better for a Minnesota fire pit? +
Gas is instant, clean and can sit close to the house and under a pergola. Wood is cheaper to build, gives a real fire, and is better for a larger gathering spot farther from the house. Many of our clients choose gas near the patio and wood out in the yard.
Do I need a permit for a fire pit or outdoor fireplace? +
A wood-burning fire pit usually does not need a permit, but most Twin Cities cities have rules on size, setbacks from structures and property lines, and burning. A gas line requires a permit pulled by a licensed gas fitter, and a tall masonry fireplace may need a building permit and footings.
Does a fire feature extend the outdoor season in Minnesota? +
It is the single feature that does the most. A fire pit or fireplace makes a patio usable in April and into November, which adds two to three months to a season that is otherwise short. Pair it with a pergola or roof and the space works in light rain and snow.
Can a fire pit go on a paver patio? +
Yes. We build fire pits and fireplaces on the same deep, compacted base as the patio so everything moves together through freeze-thaw. Wood-burning pits get a fire-rated liner, and gas features get a properly vented burner enclosure.
