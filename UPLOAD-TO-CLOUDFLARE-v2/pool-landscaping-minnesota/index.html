Pool Landscaping

Pool Landscaping and Pool Decks in Minnesota

A pool installer leaves you with a pool, a strip of coping and a yard of dirt. Everything else, the deck, the fence, the grading that keeps water out of the pool, the plantings that do not drop leaves in it, the lighting and the shade, is landscape work. We design and build all of it as one project, timed so the backyard is done the summer the pool goes in.

Most of our pool backyards are built alongside our sister company Blaiser Pools, a Latham fiberglass dealer, which is why the hand-off is seamless: our crew is on site the day the pool is set. <a href="/projects/woodbury-poolside-on-the-hill/">Poolside on the Hill</a> in Woodbury is the clearest example, a porcelain deck, boulder steps, fence, plantings and lighting finished the same season, and the homeowners' review below says exactly why they hired us. If your pool is coming from another installer, we can still design and build everything around it and coordinate grades and schedule with their crew.

The deck is the biggest decision. We install large-format concrete pavers and porcelain for pool decks because both tolerate freeze-thaw, can be reset if a section ever settles, and come in light, textured, slip-resistant finishes. From there it is grading so surface water runs away from the pool, a code-compliant fence with self-closing gates, low-litter plantings that screen the neighbors, and lighting that makes the pool the best room in the house after dark.

What Pool Landscaping Includes

  • Paver or porcelain pool deck and coping transitions
  • Grading and drainage so water runs away from the pool
  • Code-compliant fence and self-closing gates
  • Low-litter plantings and privacy screening
  • Lighting for the deck, steps and fence line
  • Pergola, cabana, fire table or outdoor kitchen
Why It Matters

Why the Yard Should Be Designed With the Pool

The pool sets the grades, the deck sets the fence line, the fence sets where the gates and the equipment go, and all of it decides whether rain runs into the pool or away from it. When the landscape is designed after the pool is in, you inherit those decisions. When it is designed with the pool, the deck is the right size, the steps land where people walk, the plantings screen the neighbors without dropping leaves in the water, and the yard is done the same summer instead of sitting as dirt for a season.

What Affects the Cost

What Landscaping Around a Pool Costs

Finishing the yard around a new pool typically lands between $75,000 and $150,000 for the deck, fence, grading, plantings and lighting; Poolside on the Hill in Woodbury was about $120,000 with a porcelain deck and boulder steps. A lighter scope where the deck was already in, like The Backyard Barn in Stillwater at about $40,000, is less; a full outdoor-living build with pergola, kitchen and fire feature, like The Home Resort in Shakopee at about $230,000, is more. The pool itself typically runs around $130,000 and is separate.

  • Deck size and materialPorcelain and large-format pavers cost more per square foot than basic concrete pavers; deck area is the biggest line.
  • GradeA sloped lot means walls and steps behind the pool.
  • Fence lengthRequired at any size; aluminum fence is priced by the foot and by the number of gates.
  • FeaturesPergola, cabana, fire table, kitchen and lighting each add to the base scope.

FAQ

Common Pool Landscaping Questions

My pool installer only does the pool. Can you do everything else? +
Yes. The deck, fence, grading, walls, plantings, lighting and shade are all landscape work, and it is what we do on every pool backyard. Most of ours are built alongside our sister company Blaiser Pools; if your pool is coming from another installer we coordinate grades and schedule with their crew so the yard is finished the same season.
What is the best pool deck material for Minnesota? +
Large-format concrete pavers and porcelain pavers are the two we install most. Both tolerate freeze-thaw, can be reset if a section ever settles, and come in light, textured, slip-resistant finishes. Porcelain costs more and gives the cleanest, most stain-resistant surface; see our porcelain page.
How much does landscaping around a pool cost? +
Typically $75,000 to $150,000 for the deck, fence, grading, plantings and lighting around a new pool, with lighter scopes around $40,000 and full outdoor-living builds above $200,000. The pool itself, around $130,000, is separate. Our pool landscaping guide breaks it down.
What plants work around a pool? +
Ornamental grasses, hydrangeas, boxwood and upright evergreens: plants that screen and soften without dropping leaves, needles or fruit in the water. We keep large deciduous trees away from the deck and carry lawn right to the deck edge where people walk barefoot.
Does the yard really get done the same season as the pool? +
That is the goal on every project and it is what the Woodbury homeowners above hired us for. Our crew starts the deck base as soon as the shell is set and backfilled, and the fence, plantings and lighting follow in sequence. Weather and permit timing can shift it, so we give you a realistic schedule up front.
Do I need a fence around a pool in Minnesota? +
Yes. Cities require a barrier around any inground pool, typically at least 4 feet high with self-closing, self-latching gates, plus setbacks and electrical permits for the equipment. Our pool rules guide explains what to expect city by city.
