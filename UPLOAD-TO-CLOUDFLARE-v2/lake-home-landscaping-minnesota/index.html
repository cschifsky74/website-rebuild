Lake Home Landscaping

Lake Home Landscaping in Minnesota and Western Wisconsin

A lake lot gives you the best view in the county and the hardest site to build on: a slope to the water, setbacks from the shoreline, limits on hard surface, and a yard that has to look right from the lake as well as from the house. We design and build lake home landscapes across the east metro and western Wisconsin, from terraced walls and stairs to the water to pools and patios that fit inside the rules.

White Bear Lake · St. Croix · Western Wisconsin Lakes · Shoreland Rules Handled

Two projects explain how we work on the water. On White Bear Lake, <a href="/projects/birchwood-village-lakefront/">Terraced on White Bear Lake</a> took a steep, unusable slope and turned it into tiered natural-stone walls, level lawn and planting terraces, stainless cable railings that keep the view open, and stairs that step all the way down to the water. On a wooded lake lot in Shakopee, <a href="/projects/shakopee-sustainable-modern/">The Sustainable Modern</a> set a fiberglass pool and tiered walls into a blank slope above the lake, inside the lake setbacks and around a septic system and mature trees the homeowners expected would force them to compromise.

The rules are part of the design. Shoreland ordinances limit how much of a lot can be hard surface, set structure setbacks from the ordinary high water level, and control what can be cleared in the shore impact zone; anything at or below the water line goes through the DNR, and the St. Croix riverway has its own stricter standards. Requirements vary by city and county, so we confirm them for your lot before we draw, and we design the walls, patios and plantings to pass review the first time.

Why It Matters

Why Lake Lots Need a Design-Build Team

On a normal lot you can build a patio and worry about the slope later. On a lake lot everything is connected: the walls set the grades, the grades set where the pool and patio can go, the setbacks and impervious limits cap all of it, and the DNR and the city both get a say. Splitting that between a wall contractor, a pool company and a landscaper is how lake projects go over budget and miss a season. We design the whole property in 3D, carry the permits, and build it with one crew that is used to working on a slope above the water.

How We Work

How We Approach a Lake Property

Site and Rules Review

We walk the lot, measure the grades, find the ordinary high water level and the setbacks, and pull the shoreland rules for your city or county.

3D Design

Walls, stairs, patio, pool and plantings designed together so you see the yard from the house and from the lake before anything is built.

Permits and Engineering

Shoreland and building permits, DNR review if anything touches the water, and engineered wall designs where height requires them.

Build From the Water Up

Walls and grading first, then stairs and railings, then patio, pool and plantings, with erosion control in place the whole time.

Finish and Walkthrough

Lighting, lawn and shoreline plantings, and a walkthrough on care, especially for the buffer plantings near the water.

What Affects the Cost

What a Lake Home Landscape Costs

Lake projects are almost always full-property work. Terraced on White Bear Lake was about $400,000 for tiered natural-stone walls, cable railings, stairs to the water, a paver driveway and plantings. The Sustainable Modern in Shakopee was about $380,000 for a fiberglass pool, tiered walls, fire table, steps, fencing, plantings and lighting on a lake lot, phased to fit the budget.

A complete backyard in the Twin Cities generally runs $100,000 to $300,000; lake lots land toward the top because of the walls and the slope. Retaining walls alone run $65 to $120 per square foot of face depending on material, with engineering and permits adding $2,500 to $5,000 on walls over about 4 feet.

  • Slope and wall heightThe steeper the lot, the more wall, and walls over about 4 feet need engineering.
  • AccessGetting equipment and stone down to the water on a tight lake lot drives labor.
  • Shoreland limitsImpervious caps can mean permeable pavers or a smaller footprint; DNR work adds review time.
  • ScopeA pool, railings, lighting and shoreline plantings each add to the walls-and-stairs base.

FAQ

Common Lake Home Landscaping Questions

What are the rules for landscaping on a lake in Minnesota? +
Shoreland zoning, administered by your city or county under DNR standards, limits impervious surface on the lot, sets structure setbacks from the ordinary high water level, and restricts clearing in the shore impact zone near the water. Work at or below the water line needs DNR approval, and properties on the Lower St. Croix have stricter riverway rules. We confirm the specific requirements for your lot before we design.
Can I put a pool on a lake lot? +
Often, yes, if it fits inside the setbacks and the impervious-surface cap, and if the equipment and fence can be placed to code. The Sustainable Modern in Shakopee is a fiberglass pool on a lake lot that the owners expected would not allow one. Our pool rules guide covers what cities ask for.
How do you handle a steep slope down to the water? +
With tiered retaining walls that hold the grade and create level terraces, stairs with landings, and plantings and drainage that stop erosion. Walls over about 4 feet get an engineered design. Terraced on White Bear Lake is the example: a steep, unusable slope turned into lawn, gardens and stairs to the dock.
Do you replace old wood retaining walls and stairs on lake lots? +
Yes. Rotting timber walls and stairs are one of the most common lake projects we get, including a full demolition and rebuild at a western Wisconsin lake home. See our timber wall replacement page.
Which lakes do you work on? +
White Bear Lake, the Stillwater and Hudson stretch of the St. Croix, the lakes of the northeast metro including Mahtomedi and Dellwood, and lake homes across western Wisconsin. We are based in Lake Elmo and also work from Shakopee.
