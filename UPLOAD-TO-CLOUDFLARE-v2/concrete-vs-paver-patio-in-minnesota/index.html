Concrete vs. Paver Patio in Minnesota: Cost and Which Lasts Longer [2026 Data]

In Minnesota, a poured concrete patio costs less up front, typically $10 to $20 per square foot installed, while a concrete paver patio runs $45 to $62 per square foot. But up-front price is only half the story. In our freeze-thaw climate, pavers usually last longer, repair more easily, and hold their look better, which often makes them the lower cost to own over the life of the patio.

Here is the honest side-by-side, from a licensed Minnesota contractor who installs both.

Concrete vs. paver patio at a glance

  • Poured concrete: cheaper up front ($10 to $20 / sq ft), but rigid, so it is prone to cracking, spalling, and heaving in Minnesota winters.
  • Concrete pavers: more up front ($45 to $62 / sq ft), but they flex with freeze-thaw and any settled paver can be lifted and reset.
  • Stamped concrete costs more than a plain slab (~$15 to $25+ / sq ft) but is still a rigid surface that can crack and fade.
  • Over a 30-year horizon, pavers often cost less to own thanks to durability and repairability.
  • The base underneath matters more than the surface; a poor base fails either material.
  • For resale in the Twin Cities, an intact paver patio signals quality; a cracked slab does the opposite.

Why concrete cracks here (and pavers do not)

A poured slab is one rigid piece. When the ground beneath it freezes, heaves, and thaws, the slab has nowhere to go, so it cracks. Control joints guide where those cracks form, but they cannot eliminate them, and surface spalling from de-icing salt and moisture is common on older Twin Cities slabs.

Interlocking pavers solve this differently. Instead of one rigid surface, you have dozens of small units sitting on a deep, compacted, well-drained base. They flex with ground movement, water sheds through the joints, and if a paver ever settles, it can be lifted and reset without touching the rest of the patio. That is why most patio failures we are called to fix are cracked concrete, not pavers.

When concrete still makes sense

We will be straight with you: concrete is not a bad choice for every situation. If budget is the top priority and the patio is a simple, low-visibility surface, a well-jointed concrete slab on a good base can serve you well. The key word is base, a slab poured on poorly prepared ground will crack fast no matter how good the concrete is.

When pavers are the better call

For most of the outdoor living spaces we build, pavers win: patios you gather on, spaces attached to a nice home, anywhere the look and longevity matter. They resist our winters, they are repairable, they come in porcelain and natural stone for high-end looks, and they protect resale value. Frequently Asked Questions

Is a concrete or paver patio cheaper in Minnesota? +
Poured concrete is cheaper up front, typically $10 to $20 per square foot installed versus $45 to $62 for concrete pavers. But over the life of the patio, pavers often cost less to own in Minnesota because they flex with freeze-thaw instead of cracking, and individual pavers can be reset rather than replacing a whole slab.
Do concrete patios crack in Minnesota winters? +
Poured concrete slabs are prone to cracking, spalling, and heaving in Minnesota because they are rigid and our freeze-thaw cycles move the ground underneath. Control joints help, but they cannot fully prevent it. Interlocking pavers avoid this by flexing with the ground and shedding water through their joints.
Which lasts longer, a concrete patio or pavers? +
Pavers typically last longer in our climate. A well-built paver patio can perform for 30+ years, and any settled paver can be lifted and reset. A poured slab usually shows cracking or surface spalling sooner, and repairs are far more visible and harder to make disappear.
Are pavers worth the extra cost over concrete? +
For most Twin Cities homeowners, yes. Pavers cost more up front but resist freeze-thaw damage, are repairable, and hold their appearance and resale appeal longer. Concrete is a reasonable budget choice, but it carries more risk of cracking in Minnesota and is harder to repair invisibly.
What about stamped concrete versus pavers? +
Stamped concrete costs more than a plain slab (often $15 to $25+ per square foot) and gives a decorative look, but it is still a rigid poured surface, so it can crack and the color layer can wear or fade. Pavers deliver a similar high-end look with better freeze-thaw performance and easier repairs.
