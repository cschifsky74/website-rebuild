How Much Does a Plunge Pool Cost in Minnesota? [2026 Data]

By Carson Schifsky, Owner, Schifsky Companies

A plunge pool in Minnesota costs $85,000 to $130,000 for the pool itself, installed. Because a pool is only as good as the space around it, a complete plunge pool project with patio, landscape and outdoor living usually lands between $150,000 and $260,000. A plunge pool costs less than a full-size pool, but the savings are smaller than most people expect, and the real reason to choose one is fit, not price.

Below is the 2026 breakdown from a licensed Twin Cities pool and landscape builder, with a real project number and an honest look at when a plunge pool is the right call.

Plunge pool cost at a glance

  • The plunge pool itself runs $85,000 to $130,000, installed.
  • A complete plunge pool and backyard project runs $150,000 to $260,000.
  • A full-size pool for comparison runs around $130,000 for the pool alone.
  • Heating adds roughly $5,000 to $10,000 and is what makes a plunge pool worth it in Minnesota.
  • Equipment, electrical, permits and fencing cost about the same as a full-size pool.
  • Our Still Waters project in Mahtomedi, a compact fiberglass pool with full landscape, came in at $260,000.
  • Best value window: design over winter and book by February for a summer swim.

What is a plunge pool?

A plunge pool is a compact pool, typically 10 to 20 feet long and 8 to 12 feet wide, built for cooling off, lounging on a ledge, and soaking rather than swimming laps. Most are 4 to 6 feet deep. The best ones are heated, which turns them into a large spa in the shoulder seasons and is the single biggest reason plunge pools make sense in Minnesota.

Plunge pools come in fiberglass shells and vinyl-liner builds, the same two systems we use for full-size pools. Our guide to fiberglass vs. vinyl pools in Minnesota covers that decision.

Why a plunge pool is not half the price

People assume a pool half the size costs half as much. It does not. The shell is smaller and the hole is smaller, which saves real money, but the pump, filter, heater, automation, electrical service, gas line, permits, inspections and code-required fencing cost about the same whether the pool is 12 feet long or 36. Those fixed costs are why the pool itself still starts around $85,000.

Where a plunge pool truly saves is around the pool. A smaller pool needs less deck, less grading and less of the yard, so the complete project can land well under a full-size pool project on the same lot.

What actually drives the price

Heating and automation are the first lever. A heater, a cover and app-based automation make a plunge pool usable from April to October and are worth every dollar in our climate. Features are the second: a tanning ledge, bench seating, jets, LED lighting and a spillover spa each add to the number. The surround is the third and the biggest: the patio, walls, plantings, lighting, and any fire feature or pergola that turns a pool into a backyard. Site conditions round it out: access for the excavator and crane, soil, grade and how far utilities have to run.

A real Minnesota plunge-style pool

Our Still Waters project in Mahtomedi is a compact fiberglass pool set into a complete landscape near White Bear Lake, with hardscape, plantings and outdoor living designed around it. Permits, fencing and the build season

Every inground pool in Minnesota needs a building permit, an electrical permit and a code-compliant barrier, and lakeshore lots may need watershed review. We handle all of it. The build season runs roughly late April through October, and pool shells and equipment have lead times, so homeowners who design over the winter and book by February are the ones swimming by July.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a plunge pool cost in Minnesota? +
A plunge pool in Minnesota runs $85,000 to $130,000 for the pool itself, installed. Because a pool is only as good as the space around it, a complete plunge pool project with patio, landscape and outdoor living usually lands between $150,000 and $260,000.
What is a plunge pool? +
A plunge pool is a compact, usually deeper pool built for cooling off, lounging and soaking rather than swimming laps. Most run 10 to 20 feet long and 8 to 12 feet wide, and many are heated so they double as a spa in spring and fall.
Is a plunge pool cheaper than a regular pool? +
Somewhat. A plunge pool saves on the shell, excavation and the amount of patio around it, so the pool itself runs $85,000 to $130,000 versus around $130,000 for a full-size pool. The savings are real but smaller than people expect, because the equipment, plumbing, electrical and permits cost about the same regardless of size.
Can you heat a plunge pool in Minnesota? +
Yes, and most of our clients do. A heater lets a plunge pool run warm enough to use as a spa in April, May, September and October, which is the main reason to choose one in our climate. Its smaller volume also heats faster and costs less to keep warm than a full-size pool.
Do plunge pools work in small Twin Cities backyards? +
That is exactly where they shine. A plunge pool fits on lots where a full-size pool would eat the whole yard, and it leaves room for a patio, fire feature and plantings so the backyard still feels like a backyard.
Do I need a permit for a plunge pool in Minnesota? +
Yes. Every inground pool in Minnesota requires a building permit, an electrical permit and, in most cities, a fence or barrier that meets code. Lakeshore properties may also need watershed review. We handle the permits and inspections as part of the project.
