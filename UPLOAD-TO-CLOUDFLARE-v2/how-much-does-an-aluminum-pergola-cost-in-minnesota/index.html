How Much Does an Aluminum Pergola Cost in Minnesota? [2026 Data]

By Carson Schifsky, Owner, Schifsky Companies

A motorized louvered aluminum pergola in Minnesota costs $25,000 to $60,000 installed. A fixed-roof aluminum pergola runs $15,000 to $30,000, and a cedar pergola, for comparison, runs $12,000 to $30,000. The right choice comes down to how much you want the structure to do: a cedar pergola gives you shade and a look, while a louvered aluminum pergola gives you a roof that opens and closes, lighting, screens and heat, and no maintenance for decades.

Below is the 2026 breakdown from a licensed Twin Cities design-build contractor, with real project examples and an honest aluminum vs. wood comparison.

Aluminum pergola cost at a glance

  • Motorized louvered aluminum pergola: $25,000 to $60,000 installed.
  • Fixed-roof aluminum pergola: $15,000 to $30,000 installed.
  • Cedar or wood pergola for comparison: $12,000 to $30,000 installed, plus staining every 2 to 3 years.
  • Integrated LED lighting adds roughly $1,500 to $4,000; zip screens $2,500 to $8,000; heaters $1,500 to $5,000.
  • A louvered roof turns a pergola into a covered outdoor room usable in rain and full sun.
  • Frost footings and a proper patio base are part of the price and part of why it lasts.
  • Lead time is 4 to 8 weeks, so we order early for a summer install.

What actually drives the price

Size is the first lever. A 12-by-14 foot pergola over a dining table is the low end of the range. A 16-by-24 that covers a kitchen and lounge is the high end, and very large or multi-bay structures go beyond it.

Attached or freestanding is the second. Attaching to the house saves a pair of posts but needs a ledger detail that ties into the structure properly. Freestanding pergolas need four or more footings but can go anywhere in the yard, including out by a pool.

Options are the third and where budgets climb. Motorized louvers are the big one. Integrated LED lighting, zip screens on one to four sides, privacy walls, ceiling fans, infrared heaters and rain sensors each add to the number, and each one adds evenings you can spend outside.

Footings and the patio round it out. A pergola sits on frost footings below the frost line so it does not heave, and it almost always lands on a new or existing paver patio, so the two are designed together.

Aluminum vs. wood in Minnesota

Cedar is beautiful the day it goes up. Then Minnesota gets to work on it. Sun grays it, freeze-thaw checks and twists it, and it needs stain every two or three years to hold its color, which most homeowners stop doing by year five. A cedar pergola is a fine choice if you want a rustic look and partial shade at the lowest cost.

Powder-coated aluminum does not rot, warp, split or need stain, and the louvered systems we install are engineered for Minnesota snow load. More importantly, a louvered roof does something wood cannot: it closes in rain, opens to the sky at night, and tilts to block low afternoon sun. Add screens and a heater and the pergola becomes a room you use from April to November. Over the life of the structure, aluminum is usually the better value, and it is the only choice we recommend over an outdoor kitchen or as part of a pool project.

Real Twin Cities pergolas

Our Woodbury Poolside project, a $425,000 full backyard, frames a new pool and paver patio with a louvered aluminum pergola over a covered outdoor living space, so the family has shade at the pool and cover for dinner. Life on the Fairway in Woodbury, at $430,000, uses a pergola to anchor the outdoor living side of a full pool and landscape build. In both, the pergola is what turns the patio into a room.

Permits and the build season

Most Twin Cities cities require a building permit for a pergola over a certain footprint or for any pergola attached to the house, and the electrical for motors, lighting and heaters needs a permit pulled by a licensed electrician. We handle the permits as part of the project. Because pergola systems have a four to eight week lead time, the smartest move is to finalize the design over winter so the structure arrives the week the patio is ready.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an aluminum pergola cost in Minnesota? +
A motorized louvered aluminum pergola in Minnesota runs $25,000 to $60,000 installed, depending on size, whether it is attached or freestanding, and options like lighting, screens and heaters. A fixed-roof aluminum pergola runs $15,000 to $30,000.
Is an aluminum pergola worth it compared to wood? +
For most Twin Cities homeowners, yes. A cedar pergola costs less up front, at $12,000 to $30,000, but needs staining every two to three years and will eventually check, warp and gray. Aluminum never rots, never needs stain, and a louvered roof turns the pergola into a covered room in rain or full sun.
Can an aluminum pergola handle Minnesota snow? +
Yes. The louvered systems we install are engineered for snow load and open the louvers to shed snow. The structure is set on frost footings so it does not heave, and the aluminum is powder coated so it does not corrode from salt or moisture.
Do I need a permit for a pergola in Minnesota? +
Usually. Most Twin Cities cities require a building permit for a pergola over a certain footprint or any pergola attached to the house, and electrical for lighting, heaters or motors requires a permit from a licensed electrician. We handle the permits as part of the project.
What can you add to an aluminum pergola? +
Motorized louvers, integrated LED lighting, zip screens for bugs and shade, privacy walls, ceiling fans, infrared heaters and rain sensors that close the roof automatically. Screens and heaters are the two additions that most extend the season in Minnesota.
How long does it take to install an aluminum pergola? +
Installation itself takes a few days to a week once the footings and patio are in. Lead time on the pergola system is usually four to eight weeks, so we order it early in the project timeline so it arrives when the patio is ready.
