Paver Patios & Hardscape

Paver Patios & Hardscape in the Twin Cities

A great patio is the foundation of an outdoor space. We design and build durable paver and natural-stone patios, walkways, and steps engineered to handle Minnesota’s freeze-thaw winters.

Every patio starts with proper base preparation and grading - the part you don’t see is what keeps it looking right for decades. A patio is only as good as the base beneath it, so we excavate to the right depth, build up a compacted aggregate base, and grade for drainage before a single paver is set.

From there we lay paver or natural stone in the pattern, color, and layout that fits your home, lock it in with polymeric sand, and finish the edges so nothing shifts. The result is a patio that still looks new after a decade of Minnesota winters.

Why It Matters

Why the Base Matters More Than the Pavers

Most failed patios in Minnesota don’t fail because of the pavers - they fail because of what’s underneath. Freeze-thaw cycles heave anything that wasn’t built on a properly compacted, well-drained base. We over-build the part you’ll never see so the part you do see stays flat, even, and tight for decades. It’s the difference between a patio you repair in five years and one you enjoy for thirty.

FAQ

Common Paver Patios & Hardscape Questions

Will a paver patio heave or crack in Minnesota winters? +
Not when it’s built right. Interlocking pavers are designed to flex with freeze-thaw movement, and our deep, compacted, well-drained base is what keeps the whole surface stable. If anything ever settles, individual pavers can be lifted and reset - unlike a poured slab.
Paver patio vs. poured concrete, which is better? +
Pavers cost a bit more up front but typically outlast and outperform poured concrete here: they resist cracking, are easy to repair, and offer far more design options. Poured concrete is cheaper initially but tends to crack with our freeze-thaw cycles and is hard to fix invisibly.
Do paver patios need sealing or much maintenance? +
They’re low-maintenance. An occasional rinse and re-sanding of the joints keeps them looking sharp, and sealing is optional - it deepens the color and makes cleanup easier but isn’t required.
What does a paver patio cost? +
It depends on size, materials, site prep, and features like seat walls or fire pits. We give you an honest range at your free consultation and a firm, itemized quote once the design is set.
